For years, I couldn't figure it out. I was responsive, polite, and good at my job. Still, couples slipped away while my competitors booked gig after gig. What was I doing wrong?

The answer came from a role-play with a DJ friend. We staged a mock sales call for laughs. Halfway through my painfully awkward side of the dialogue, it hit me. I wasn't talking like a DJ. I was talking like my day job. I had been corporatized.

My messages were polished, efficient, and sterile: services, pricing, repeat. I was treating clients like contracts instead of people whose once-in-a-lifetime night I'd get to be part of. That stung, but it was the truth.

So I stopped running my business like a business.

Go ahead and roll your eyes. I still believe in sound economics. But I rebuilt my approach around emotional intelligence, and it changed everything. When a client opens with "How much?", the instinct is to talk numbers. Don't. Build the relationship, and the bookings follow. I'm busier, more connected, and more energized than ever.

## 10 ways to bring more heart to your business

1. Remember you're a customer too. Treat people the way you want to be treated.

2. Be personal, not transactional. If a client is only price shopping, ask "How did you two meet?" or "Describe your perfect party." Bring it back to what matters.

3. Treat every client like the only client. Templates feel efficient. They also feel cold, and they can leave people feeling misunderstood.

4. People book people they like. Remember details. Ask about the vacation. Share your favorite restaurant. Be a human, not a brand.

5. Let your social media be real. Clients check it to see who they're hiring. Perfect feels fake, so leave a few bad hair days in there. Stay professional, but let your personality show.

6. Use humor. A good joke wins over a skeptic. Professional doesn't have to mean stiff.

7. Meet face to face. FaceTime and email are convenient, but nothing beats eye contact and a friendly face.

8. Personalize at least two things in every email. Their name and event date don't count.

9. Never bad-mouth a competitor. Give credit where it's due. It shows you're honest and confident.

10. Don't fear the silence. Let the conversation breathe. Nobody wants a sales pitch. Listen like you mean it.

Clients can spot a pushy close from a mile away. Be a better DJ than that.
